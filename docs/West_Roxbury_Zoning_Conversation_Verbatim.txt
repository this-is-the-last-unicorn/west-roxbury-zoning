## Neighborhood Zoning / “What Happens to My Lot?” — Product Spec v0.1

### 1. Product vision

Build a public-facing website that makes Boston zoning changes understandable **at the individual property and neighborhood level**.

The fundamental user question is:

> **“What does this zoning proposal actually mean for my house, my lot, and my neighborhood?”**

Instead of requiring residents to understand zoning articles, GIS layers, dimensional tables, planning terminology, or city presentations, a resident should be able to enter an address and receive a plain-English explanation of:

**Current property → proposed zoning → what changes → what could actually be built → how meaningful the change is.**

Initially this is centered on the current Boston residential rezoning process, particularly **West Roxbury**, with the analysis we've already been doing around **RD-2 vs. RD-3**.

But I think we should explicitly decide whether the product is ultimately:

**A.** a West Roxbury advocacy/information site built for this particular rezoning fight, or  
**B.** a reusable Boston zoning-transparency platform whose first deployment happens to be West Roxbury.

That distinction affects basically everything downstream.

---

# 2. Core problem

The City's zoning materials tell residents **what district is being proposed**, but they don't easily answer the practical questions residents actually have:

- What zoning is proposed for **my property**?
- What can be built there today?
- What could be built under the proposal?
- How many units could theoretically exist?
- Could another building be added?
- How do setbacks change?
- How does lot coverage change?
- What happens to height?
- What happens to parking requirements?
- Does an existing nonconformity become conforming?
- Are there bonus-unit or other development opportunities?
- Why was my parcel assigned RD-3 while another similar parcel was assigned RD-2?
- What would the aggregate effect be across my street or neighborhood?

The City's underlying proposed-zoning map itself contains RD-2, RD-3, RD-4 and RD-6 classifications, among others. fileciteturn0file2L115-L181

The website converts that information from **planning language into property consequences**.

# 8. Data confidence

This needs to be unusually transparent because we're dealing with parcel-level estimates.

Each property should internally — and possibly publicly — carry a **confidence rating**.

The current model already penalizes things like:

- older assessor data
- older parcel/building geometry
- non-parcel-specific current zoning
- unofficial proposed zoning
- low-confidence frontage
- GIS-estimated setbacks
- unknown ADU status
- estimated parking
- conflicting sources
- missing critical fields fileciteturn0file4L262-L284

The public UX could say:

**Data confidence: High**

or

**Some measurements are estimated → Learn more**

rather than dumping methodology onto normal users.

But researchers/power users should be able to drill all the way down.

# 10. RD-2 vs. RD-3 analysis

This remains a central analytical feature.

We have repeatedly come back to two questions that the site/research needs to be capable of answering:

**1. What objective methodology was used to assign every parcel to RD-2, RD-3, RD-4 or RD-6?**

**2. If the goal is to reflect existing neighborhood character, what characteristics distinguish an RD-2 area from an RD-3 area?**

The site could eventually let a user compare:

**My parcel → nearby parcels → proposed classification → observable parcel/building characteristics.**

That makes it possible to identify areas where the proposed designation appears inconsistent with comparable blocks.

This may become one of the most powerful parts of the site.

# 12. Sources and methodology

This should be a first-class part of the site rather than buried legalese.

Sources should prioritize official Boston materials, particularly the appropriate Boston planning/zoning research sources.

Our current pipeline already connects directly to the official **Proposed New Zoning Subdistricts** layer and applies the published `Comments = 'CH'` filter. fileciteturn0file0L11-L26

Every major result should be traceable.

Something like:

**Where did this come from?**

- Proposed zoning → Boston official GIS
- Parcel geometry → source/date
- Building footprint → source/date
- Assessor information → source/date
- Zoning requirements → Article / section
- Calculated setback → methodology + confidence

The City's map currently sends users from the zoning designation to district-specific explanatory material. fileciteturn0file3L206-L222 We can retain those official links while giving users a substantially clearer explanation.

# 13. Accuracy / release gate

This part is important: **the current analytical build is not ready to publish.**

The existing release-readiness check explicitly says **DO NOT PUBLISH**. Current issues include missing current/proposed zoning for some parcels, malformed compliance fields, missing app JSON outputs, duplicate-address issues, and detailed zoning rules that haven't yet been completely verified against the legal text. fileciteturn0file6L362-L395

Also, although the setback methodology has improved to use nearby street centerlines and address context, setbacks remain GIS estimates rather than surveys. fileciteturn0file4L247-L256

So I would put an explicit release requirement in Zac's spec:

**No parcel-level conclusion is published until its underlying zoning rule has been verified against the operative/draft legal text and the automated QA suite passes.**

# 14. Possible product architecture

I think there are four layers:

**Layer 1 — Consumer UX**

Address → “What happens to my lot?”

**Layer 2 — Exploration**

Interactive neighborhood map.

**Layer 3 — Analysis**

Neighborhood statistics, RD comparisons, impact analysis.

**Layer 4 — Evidence**

Sources, methodology, raw calculations, confidence and downloadable data.

Most residents live almost entirely in Layer 1.

We should not make them feel like they're opening ArcGIS.

# West Roxbury Zoning Analysis
## V1 Product Specification

**Status:** Working V1 Product Spec  
**Geography:** West Roxbury, Boston, Massachusetts  
**Working title:** West Roxbury Zoning Analysis

---

# 1. PRODUCT VISION

West Roxbury Zoning Analysis is an independent civic-information website that helps West Roxbury residents understand what Boston's proposed Neighborhood Housing zoning changes mean for:

1. their property;
2. their block;
3. their street;
4. their sub-neighborhood/community; and
5. West Roxbury overall.

The fundamental question the product should answer is:

**“What does this rezoning proposal actually mean for me, my property, my block, and my neighborhood?”**

The product should translate technical zoning rules and City datasets into factual, property-specific information that a normal homeowner can understand.

It should not require residents to understand zoning terminology, interpret GIS layers, compare dimensional tables, or read the zoning amendment themselves.

The core experience is:

**Enter an address → understand what exists → understand what today's zoning allows → understand what the proposed zoning allows → understand what could actually change on this particular property.**

---

# 2. POSITIONING AND NEUTRALITY

The site must be as factually neutral as possible.

It is:

- an independent civic-data project;
- not affiliated with or endorsed by the City of Boston;
- not an advocacy organization;
- not a development-feasibility tool;
- not a property-acquisition tool;
- not a developer-prospecting tool.

The site may sometimes *feel* critical of the proposal because it clearly presents the magnitude of potential changes. That is acceptable.

The site should not minimize a change in order to appear neutral.

Likewise, it should not exaggerate a theoretical possibility in order to make the proposal appear more consequential.

The goal is to **call the facts what they are**.

For example:

> Existing house height: 25 ft  
> Proposed maximum: 35 ft  
> Potential outcome: An additional story may be possible.

That is factual.

The site should NOT say:

> This proposal could allow developers to tower over your neighborhood.

Likewise, it should NOT obscure the 10-foot increase merely because a homeowner is unlikely to build it tomorrow.

---

# 3. CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE: CONSEQUENCES, NOT OPPORTUNITIES

The site exists to explain what zoning changes permit.

It must **not package those changes as development opportunities**.

Appropriate:

- “The proposed zoning increases maximum height by 10 feet.”
- “The proposed rules could allow approximately 500 additional square feet of building footprint.”
- “Another dwelling unit may be permitted.”
- “A detached structure of approximately X square feet may fit under the proposed dimensional rules.”
- “An additional unit would not appear possible because [verified constraint].”

Not appropriate:

- “This property has high redevelopment potential.”
- “Best lots for additional units.”
- “Properties with the greatest zoning upside.”
- “Most profitable redevelopment opportunities.”
- “Underutilized parcels.”
- redevelopment ROI or acquisition analysis.

There should be **no public development-potential heat map, bulk opportunity search, profitability model, or parcel ranking**.

---

# 4. V1 GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE

V1 covers:

**West Roxbury only.**

Do not build Jamaica Plain, Roslindale, Hyde Park, or broader Boston coverage into the V1 user experience.

The technical architecture should not be unnecessarily hostile to future expansion, but expansion is not a V1 requirement.

---

# 5. PRIMARY USER

The primary user is:

**A West Roxbury homeowner/resident who has heard about the rezoning proposal and wants to know what it means for their property and immediate community.**

The site should assume:

- little or no zoning expertise;
- limited patience for technical planning terminology;
- strong interest in their own home first;
- secondary interest in their immediate block and neighborhood;
- a desire to understand rather than conduct professional land-use analysis.

No login is required.

Any West Roxbury address should be searchable.

# 10. CORE PROPERTY COMPARISON

The primary analytical structure should use four conceptual columns:

| Metric | Today's Law | Today's Reality | Proposed Law | Potential Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Working labels can be UX-tested later, but the distinction must remain.

## Today's Law

What current zoning legally permits/requires.

## Today's Reality

What physically/legalistically exists on the property today.

## Proposed Law

What the proposed zoning would permit/require.

## Potential Outcome

What the proposed rules appear to enable on this particular property after applying known physical and legal constraints.

Potential Outcome is NOT:

- a prediction of owner behavior;
- an economic feasibility analysis;
- a probability of redevelopment;
- a recommendation;
- a developer opportunity score.

# 11. REQUIRED PROPERTY METRICS

The table should mirror the metrics actually regulated by the applicable zoning proposal rather than inventing new zoning concepts.

Core expected metrics include:

### Dwelling units
- Today's Law: units legally permitted.
- Today's Reality: verified/legal existing dwelling units only.
- Proposed Law: units permitted.
- Potential Outcome: additional units that appear physically/legalistically possible, only when sufficiently certain.

Do not infer unverified dwelling units from building appearance.

### Height
Separate row measured in feet.

### Stories
Separate from height.

Half-stories must be supported.

Example:

**2½ stories**

Do not round these to whole stories.

### Building floor plate / footprint
Follow the proposal's actual legal definition.

Where data permits, internally distinguish:

- enclosed building footprint;
- covered porches/balconies or other exterior areas counted by zoning;
- total zoning floor plate.

Do not tell a homeowner that their “house” occupies 1,500 sq. ft. if 250 sq. ft. of the zoning floor plate is actually covered porch.

Where useful:

**Enclosed building footprint:** X sq. ft.  
**Covered exterior area counted by zoning:** Y sq. ft.  
**Total zoning floor plate:** Z sq. ft.

### Lot coverage
Always show:

- square feet; AND
- percentage of total lot.

Example:

**1,500 sq. ft. / 18%**

### Permeable/open lot area
Homeowner-facing terminology:

**Permeable/open lot area**

Show:

- square feet; AND
- percentage of lot.

Technical zoning definition should be available through tooltip/methodology.

### Setbacks
Mirror the proposal exactly.

If the zoning uses:

- cumulative side setback → show cumulative;
- minimum individual side setback → show that;
- separate left/right → show those.

Do not invent a different metric merely because it is easier to visualize.

### Parking

Always include parking.

Distinguish:

**Today's Law:** current zoning parking requirement  
**Today's Reality:** physical parking spaces currently present  
**Proposed Law:** proposed zoning parking requirement  
**Potential Outcome:** plain-English consequence where appropriate

### Roof/dormer rules

Treat these exactly as the zoning proposal treats them.

Do not invent a separate dormer metric if the proposal does not regulate it separately.

### Building character/type

Include where classification can be made with sufficiently high confidence.

Examples may include:

- Cape
- Colonial
- Victorian
- Ranch
- multifamily form
- etc.

If reliable classification cannot be made:

**Undetermined**

AI/model-derived classifications are acceptable only at high confidence and should be labeled as estimated rather than official City classifications.

# 13. EXISTING CONDITIONS / GRANDFATHERING COMMUNICATION

This is a critical UX requirement.

Residents must NEVER look at the comparison table and conclude:

> “The proposed zoning means I have to change my existing house.”

Anywhere current conditions are compared against current or proposed dimensional standards, make it **extremely obvious** that a zoning change generally governs what may be permitted going forward and does not automatically require an existing homeowner to alter their house.

Use clear explanatory language such as:

> **Your existing home can remain as it is.**  
> Proposed zoning changes what may be permitted going forward; it does not mean your home must be rebuilt to match the new dimensional standards.

Exact legal language must be verified before publication.

## Existing conditions outside current standards

Use subtle visual indicators when Today's Reality differs from Today's Law.

Do NOT casually label these properties:

- illegal;
- violations;
- noncompliant.

Existing conditions may be lawful pre-existing nonconformities.

Possible statuses:

- Meets applicable standard
- Existing condition differs from standard
- Existing condition may remain
- Needs verification

Color may reinforce these states but must never be the only indicator.

# 15. POTENTIAL OUTCOME ENGINE

This is one of the primary reasons the product exists.

The system should go beyond merely displaying zoning tables and explain **what the change actually enables on the searched property**.

However, it must avoid fantastical or edge-case redevelopment scenarios.

Appropriate examples:

> You could potentially increase the building height by approximately 10 feet, which is roughly one additional story.

> The proposed floor-plate rules could permit approximately 500 additional square feet of ground-level building area.

> A garage of approximately 500 square feet may fit within the proposed dimensional rules.

> An additional dwelling unit does not appear feasible because [specific verified constraint].

The engine should favor **obvious, conservative applications of the proposed rules** rather than searching for clever combinations that maximize development.

# 16. POTENTIAL OUTCOME CONSTRAINTS

Potential Outcome should account for relevant physical/legal constraints including, where reliably available:

- lot geometry;
- existing building footprint;
- applicable setbacks;
- floor-plate limitations;
- lot coverage;
- permeable/open-area requirement;
- height;
- stories;
- parking rules;
- location of existing structures;
- detached structures;
- applicable zoning restrictions;
- other parcel-specific dimensional constraints.

Do NOT use:

- speculative redevelopment economics;
- projected resale values;
- ROI;
- acquisition price;
- construction profitability.

Economic feasibility is outside V1.

**Needs property-specific review**
